# Design review for a larger washroom in a place of worship

**Finding.** The example room is 50 mm short in clear depth. The alarm control height meets the selected mounting-height test. The electrical drawing still needs to show where the alarm reports.

**Project evidence (illustrative).** A new place of worship first submitted plans on 2 June 2026. The Level 2 plan shows toilets and one designated larger accessible individual washroom. The project team confirms 1,850 mm clear width and 2,050 mm clear depth between finished faces. The electrical schedule shows a waterproof push-button 500 mm above floor level. An outside visual indicator appears on the drawing. Its destination, the control's contrast and its adjacent sign are unrecorded.

| Test | BCA locator | Evidence and finding |
|---|---|---|
| Room provision on this level | §5.1.3(d), p. 61 and Table 1, row 11, p. 20 | The Level 2 schedule contains one designated room. Check each applicable level separately. |
| Clear internal room size | §5.2.1(b), p. 63 and Figure 44, p. 65 | Required width × depth is at least 1,800 × 2,100 mm. The width has 50 mm to spare. The depth is 50 mm short. |
| Alarm control | §5.2.2(d), p. 63 and §§5.8.4.1–5.8.4.2, p. 81 | The specified button and its 500 mm height support those two checks. Obtain the elevation or finish schedule for contrast and the adjacent sign. |
| Indicator and destination | §5.8.4.3, p. 81 | The drawing locates a visual indicator outside the room. Obtain the wiring or cause-and-effect schedule identifying the receiving management office, fire command centre or caregiver's office. |

**BIM handoff.** The architect can identify the room by storey and space ID, then verify the two clear dimensions on the dimensioned plan or model geometry. The electrical designer can link the same room ID to the control, indicator and receiving point. A room area or an alarm symbol alone leaves the relevant check open.

**Scope.** This review covers the selected provision, size and alarm tests for Level 2. Other sanitary fittings, door requirements, the rest of the building and approvals require their own evidence.
